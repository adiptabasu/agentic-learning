# Explore a learning goal when you are unsure

Use this when you have several interests, are considering a career or job change, or know something feels missing but cannot yet name a learning goal. The aim is to understand your options, not to force a decision or produce a study plan too early.

## Start

Invoke the `learning-guide` skill, or ask:

> Read `skills/learning-guide/SKILL.md` and `personas/learning-guide.md` and follow them. I'm thinking about [your situation or interests], but I don't yet know exactly what I want to learn. Help me explore it. Ask one focused question at a time, don't assume my background or constraints, and don't settle on a goal or plan for me.

Example:

> Read `skills/learning-guide/SKILL.md` and follow it. I enjoy solving problems and working with computers, and I might want a career change, but I don't know what roles fit me. Help me explore my reasons, experience, and constraints before suggesting directions.

## During the conversation

Answer in your own terms. It is fine to say “I don't know,” “I'm not sure,” or “that doesn't fit.” If the agent moves too quickly to a recommendation, say:

> Please keep exploring before recommending a path. What is the next question that would help distinguish what I want from what I merely think I should want?

When it offers possible directions, ask it to connect them to what you actually said:

> For each option, explain what evidence from our conversation makes it worth considering, what remains uncertain, and what small next step could help me test it. Don't present a guess as a conclusion.

## Decide what happens next

When you are ready, ask for a short summary:

> Summarize the goals or directions we explored, why they matter to me, what I already know, my constraints, and what is still uncertain. Do not choose for me.

Correct the summary. Then choose one of three next steps:

- If a direction feels promising, confirm it and ask the Learning Guide to help turn it into a learning goal.
- If you need more evidence, ask for low-cost ways to explore the options before committing.
- If nothing feels right yet, pause. You can return to the exploration later; no plan or journal is required.

Example handoff prompt:

> This summary is accurate. I want to explore [chosen direction] further. Help me define what success would look like and what I should learn first. Ask about any important unknowns before using the learning-plan skill.

Do not create a journal or workspace unless you want one and have agreed on its location and Git policy.
