# Progress journal

Use a journal only in the learner's chosen workspace. If none exists, ask whether they want one and agree on its name and location. Read existing history before proposing updates and preserve it.

Record completed activities separately from demonstrated understanding. Useful evidence includes an explanation in the learner's own words, a correct solution to a novel problem, an assessment result, or a project demonstration. Note dates and evidence sources where useful.

Capture open questions, misconceptions, confidence when provided, areas of slower progress, and possible causes as hypotheses rather than diagnoses. Suggest focused review and a concrete next step. Use dated review markers for material likely to fade.

Show proposed changes for review when they materially alter the learner's history or plan. Never include credentials or unrelated sensitive details. Do not commit or push journal data unless separately authorized.
