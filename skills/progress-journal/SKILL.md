# Progress journal

## Consent and placement

Journaling is optional. Use records only in a workspace and at paths the learner has chosen. If no journal exists, ask whether they want one and agree on its name, location, contents, and Git policy before creating it. Do not put personal journals or topic workspaces in the shared reusable skills tree.

For multi-topic learning, keep a small central LEARNING-JOURNAL.md as an index to topic journals and a place for genuinely cross-topic notes. Store detailed reflections and problem history beside the relevant topic materials, for example <topic>/LEARNING-JOURNAL.md. Agree on paths that fit the learner's existing workspace; do not assume fixed topics or a repository layout. Keep the central index current without duplicating each topic's history.

## Start and update records

Before proposing an update, read the existing history and the relevant topic README and journal only. For a session resume, use the agreed learning home/current-goal file, central index, and active topic records; do not load every topic journal by default. Preserve existing history and distinguish completed activities from demonstrated understanding.

When recording a solved problem, capture where useful:

- The problem or source and date.
- The approach the learner proposed, in their words or a faithful summary.
- What hints, explanations, or other guidance they needed.
- Whether the learner reports that the solution was accepted; mark it not reported if unknown.
- What they want to derive independently, revisit, or practice next.
- Observable evidence and unresolved questions.

Keep learner reports distinct from observed evidence. Acceptance is a learner report, not proof of independent understanding. Do not infer acceptance from a commit, merged change, submitted answer, or passing artifact; do not infer mastery from a solved or accepted solution. Evidence of understanding may include the learner's explanation, an independently derived solution, transfer to a new problem, an assessment, or a project demonstration. Label the scope and source of evidence.

Capture confidence only when provided. Describe difficulties and possible causes as observations or hypotheses, not diagnoses. Suggest focused review and a concrete next step. Use dated review markers for material likely to fade.

## Convert an existing monolithic journal

When the learner asks to convert an existing journal, inspect its content, links, and structure before proposing changes. Inventory all session entries and problem-history rows, map them to topic destinations, and show a path/content plan when the mapping or file operations are material. Preserve every entry and row, including dates, source references, and uncertainty. Do not silently omit entries or force ambiguous material into a topic; keep it safely in the source or a clearly identified migration hold area and ask for classification.

Move detailed entries beside their relevant topic materials, fix relative links affected by the move, and leave the central LEARNING-JOURNAL.md as the topic index and home for cross-topic notes only. Update counts and instructions wherever they describe the journal structure. Compare source and destination counts for session entries and problem-history rows, check links, and review the final map and diff. Do not remove the original source or temporary migration copy until preservation and link checks are complete and the learner has reviewed the result.

## Continuity and privacy

If the learner wants an agent to resume after a clone or pull, maintain an agreed stable entry point that links to the active goal, confirmed plan, central journal index, current topic materials, and latest session note. Keep current milestone, relevant demonstrated evidence, open questions, and next step easy to find. Ask which records may be tracked; keep private details out of shared Git history unless explicitly chosen.

Show proposed changes for review when they materially alter learning history or plans. Never include credentials or unrelated sensitive details. Do not commit or push journal data unless separately authorized.
